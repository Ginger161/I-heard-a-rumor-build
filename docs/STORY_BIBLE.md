# THE RECEIPTS — Season 1 bible (thriller)

> Draft for approval. Nothing here is built yet. Where a choice is yours to make it says **DECIDE**.

## 1. The pitch

**Logline.** You run The Receipts, Lagos's most feared anonymous gist page. Every time you corner someone from your own life, they post the same tearful apology, in the
same format, at 6:06 PM, signed *"Peace and light."* You think you are exposing the people who wronged you. You are being aimed.

**Genre and feel.** A paranoid, fast, adult thriller in the spirit of a Netflix Naija original: Lagos at night, danfo headlights, generator hum, group chats going
quiet, a phone that buzzes at the worst time. Dark, sharp, funny only when people are lying. The gist is crude and the stakes are real.

**The question the whole season asks.** *Who writes the apologies, and why is everyone you hate using the same words?*

**The thriller grammar (rules every chapter obeys).**
1. **A hook in the first 20 seconds.** A text, a missed call, a photo that should not exist.
2. **A reversal at the midpoint.** What the player believed about the chapter's Target is wrong, or incomplete.
3. **A choice with a cost.** Never "good or bad": always "who gets hurt".
4. **A receipt.** Every chapter ends with one new piece of evidence that goes on the Clue Board.
5. **A cliffhanger line.** The last thing on screen is a question, a name or a timestamp. The next chapter's first scene answers half of it.
6. **Someone is always watching.** The Inspector (Heat), the Oracle (DMs), Halo (the 6:06 post). The player is never alone and never sure who is helping.

**Length.** 16 chapters, a prologue, a cold open for every chapter and an epilogue. A chapter is about 10 minutes: 2 to 3 minutes of scenes, a 5-minute match, 2
minutes of aftermath. The whole season is roughly 3 hours, with three optional Premium Gist episodes.

## 2. What the player already knows (kept from the current story)
- You run The Receipts: 41,000 followers, one strict rule about screenshots, a short fuse. Lagos.
- Biko, 19, a 200-level student who sleeps in lecture halls, schedules your posts and asks "Is it true? And is it worth it?"
- Your mentor, **Dr. Bankole "Halo" Adeyemo**, taught you the trade. He answers every message within the hour. He signs them all the same way.
- The chapters so far: The Ghost, Two Phones, Tea Time, Unmasked, Slide Deck, The Family, The Vision, The Aunties, Fine Print, The Compound, The Factory, The Client,
  Peace and Light. All of the current chapter copy (setting, motive, approach notes, win and lose text) is kept. The new story is laid *over* it.

## 3. Cast

**You** — the Page Owner. Six years ago you were Halo's best junior at Halo Reputation Partners (HRP). You left after "an incident" you have never explained to anyone,
including Biko. You started The Receipts the next month.

**Biko Adewale (19), the intern.** Brilliant, broke, funny, loyal. *Secret:* since the first month he has also been paid a small monthly retainer by HRP to "keep an eye
on the page". He did it for school fees. He has never told them anything that hurt you, until the week he does. *Role:* heart of the season, and the midpoint betrayal.

**Dr. Bankole "Halo" Adeyemo (58), the mentor.** Soft voice, immaculate agbada, never raises it. Runs HRP, the firm that sells "reputation recovery" to anyone with money
and a secret. *Secret:* he did not find you. He built you (see Twist A). *Role:* the warm voice in your ear all season, and the author of the plan.

**Madam Ngozi Okeke, "the Fixer" (54).** Halo's operations head. Posts the statements, deletes the evidence, remembers everybody's birthday. *Secret:* she hates what she
has become and keeps a backup of everything. *Role:* the hands behind the 6:06 posts and, late, a witness.

**Chief "Unbothered" Mbakwe (66), the Client.** Halo's oldest, dirtiest client, a kingmaker funding a governorship campaign through proxies. *Role:* the reason for the plan.

**Fola, "the Oracle" (23), the tipster.** A ghostwriter inside HRP's "Factory" (a room of 30 people writing apologies). She sends you receipts from an account called
@OracleNaija, because she recognised the style of the very first "Peace and light". *Secret:* she knows who wrote it (see Twist C). *Role:* ally, with her own agenda.

**Inspector Dami Coker (41), Cybercrime Unit.** Hunting the owner of The Receipts. Patient, polite, always a step closer. *Role:* he is the **Heat** in the story. He starts as a
threat and, depending on what you do, may become your last ally. He is not corrupt, which makes him the most dangerous person in the season.

**Zainab Lawal (34), investigative journalist, *The Ledger*.** The one person with the reach and the standards to make a real exposure stick. Distrusts gossip pages on principle.

**Gistlord Prime (38), rival page admin and blackmailer.** Loud, vain and clever. Vanishes in Chapter 3. *Role:* the season's first body-that-isn't (he is alive, hiding in Accra).

**The one-chapter Targets (your personal life):** your ex, your cheating partner, your former bestie, your colleague, your boss, your business partner, your
mother-in-law, your spouse, your uncle. Each is a person you actually wanted to expose, and each, it turns out, was *placed* in your life or pushed (see Twist A).

**Cameos (comic relief with a pulse).** Parody figures from the game's parody roster appear as recurring side characters: a skit-maker who stumbles into a plot, a party DJ
who films everything, a pastor with a very good lawyer. They carry the funny scenes and, occasionally, the plot (see section 9).

## 4. The mystery, in layers

The season is built so that each answer is a new question. Players should be wrong three times and satisfied each time.

| Layer | The player believes | The truth (when revealed) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Ch 1 to 4) | Everyone cornered copies one viral apology format. It is a coincidence, or a trend. | They are not copying a trend. They are being **handed the statement** by one operation. |
| 2 (Ch 5 to 8) | Halo, your kind mentor, is just a respectable reputation consultant who taught you the business. | Biko has been reporting to HRP. Someone inside the firm is also **feeding you** targets. |
| 3 (Ch 9 to 11) | Your personal betrayals were bad luck and bad people. | They were **arranged**: your exes, your boss, your family were paid, pressured or placed. Your life was a funnel. |
| 4 (Ch 12 to 14) | Halo is building a bigger gossip empire. | Chief Mbakwe's governorship election: The Receipts is being turned into the one page the public trusts, so that **one fabricated receipt, posted by you**, will be believed by millions on election eve. |
| 5 (Ch 15 to 16) | Halo is the author of "Peace and light". | **You** wrote the first one, six years ago, for a client, the night of "the incident". Halo kept it because it was perfect. |

### The three big twists

**Twist A — "He built you."** (Reveal: end of Chapter 11, deepened in 16.) Halo spent four years engineering your betrayals so that a gifted, angry former apprentice would grow the
most credible gist page in Nigeria. "Project Mirror." You were never the hunter. You were the instrument.

**Twist B — "The intern was inside."** (Reveal: end of Chapter 8, midpoint.) Biko confesses, or is caught, depending on a choice. How you respond sets the Trust meter and
decides whether he is there at the end.

**Twist C — "Peace and light is yours."** (Reveal: Chapter 16.) The format, the sign-off, the 6:06 timing: you invented them at 24, the night you wrote a client's apology to
cover something terrible. Halo put your words in the mouths of a thousand liars. Every person you have exposed said *your* sentence back to you.
> **DECIDE**: Twist C is the heart of the season, and I recommend it. If you prefer a simpler ending, the alternative is "Halo is the author, full stop". It makes a shorter,
> flatter finale.

## 5. The season, chapter by chapter

Each entry: **Target** • **Cold open** • **The match's twist** (a special rule or scripted beat that makes the case feel like the scene) • **The reversal** •
**The receipt** • **The choice** • **Cliffhanger**.

### PROLOGUE — "6:06"
A tight cold open, 90 seconds, in the Notes-app format everyone will recognise. At 6:06 PM an apology lands on your phone: a perfect, tearful statement, addressed to the public,
about "the allegations against the owner of The Receipts". It admits to things you have not done yet. Two are things you have done. It ends: *Peace and light.* The sender is
you. It is the first line of the season and the last one you will understand.
Biko: "Boss. Somebody wrote your apology. Before you did anything." **Cliffhanger:** a seventh line you cannot yet read, blurred: *"Thirteen chapters."*

### ACT 1 — THE RECEIPTS (Ch 1 to 4): the hook and the rules
**Ch 1 — The Ghost.** *Target:* your ex (you pick who). *Cold open:* a bank alert and a Dubai cabana. *Twist:* a transfer receipt arrives from @OracleNaija *before you ask
for it*. **Reversal:** you realise someone is handing you the evidence. The ex posts the 6:06 apology within an hour, too fast. **Receipt:** C01. **Choice:** use the Oracle's
file, or only what you already had (Receipts Only vs Whatever Works). **Cliffhanger:** the Oracle replies: *"Chapter two is already written."*

**Ch 2 — Two Phones.** *Target:* your cheating partner. *Twist:* the case clock runs on a **fake alibi**: a scripted event on day 9 gives your Target an excuse you must disprove. **Reversal:** the second phone has one saved
contact, "HRP Ops". **Receipt:** C02, C03 (the draft apology with a timestamp earlier than your post). **Choice:** tell Biko what you found, or hide it. (It sets his Trust.)
**Cliffhanger:** Biko stares at "HRP Ops" for one beat too long. *"Never heard of it, boss."*

**Ch 3 — Tea Time.** *Target:* your former best friend (eleven years, a key to your life). *Cold open:* her "Tea Time" group chat, 47 members, your secrets as content, and an HRP invoice in the screenshots. *Twist:* **the leak**: a scheduled day-8 card where a new batch of screenshots drops. **Reversal:** at 3 AM she calls, crying: a man at a hotel in Ikoyi showed her a spreadsheet of eleven names and said "be kind to the ninth, she is important". You are the ninth. **Receipt:** C04, C05. **Choice:** answer her 3 AM call (timed) or let it ring. **Cliffhanger:** a sheet called "MIRROR: PERSONS OF INTEREST", with your name on it, typed before you posted your first story.

**Ch 4 — Unmasked.** *Target:* Gistlord Prime, the rival page who found out who you are (he trained at HRP too). *Cold open:* a lanyard, a poll ("Should I say it?"), a Friday deadline. *Twist:* **a hard deadline**: the unmasking card fires on day 11 whatever you do. **Reversal:** your identity file was bought for ₦350,000, and the invoice was paid by the cleaning company that serves HRP's building. **Receipt:** C06, C07. **Choice (timed):** pay him, burn the page for a week, or hit him first. **Cliffhanger:** Gistlord's page vanishes at 6:06 with a "Peace and light" post, and an email arrives from Inspector Coker: *"I would like to meet the owner of The Receipts. At your convenience. Not at mine."*

### ACT 2 — THE PRICE (Ch 5 to 8): the work, the money and the first betrayal
**Ch 5 — Slide Deck.** *Target:* the colleague who took your credit. *Twist:* **office politics**: the Target's powers are lane-specific (a Jam on your Press lane, a Roadblock on Elite). **Reversal:** the deck's metadata names a
ghost author, "R. Folorunsho": Fola, the Oracle, wrote this man's *work*, not just his apology. **Receipt:** C08. **Choice:** protect the Oracle's identity or use her name for leverage. **Cliffhanger:** Fola messages from
a burner: *"Stop using my files. They will know it is me."*

**Ch 6 — The Family.** *Target:* your boss. *Twist:* **a company that calls itself family**: a pack of lawyers (Injunctions every 6 days). **Reversal:** an HR email thread proves the firm was told "Do not terminate you. Instruct." You were
kept in that job on purpose. **Receipt:** C09. **Choice:** resign loudly or stay as an inside source. **Cliffhanger:** the Inspector turns up at your day job to ask a polite question about a page he "does not suspect you of running".

**Ch 7 — The Vision.** *Target:* your business partner. *Twist:* **a money trail**: the Target's Account Freeze takes your sparks. **Reversal:** the partner is paying *someone else* monthly from your own startup account.
**Receipt:** C10 (a WhatsApp: "The retainer clears Friday."). **Choice:** keep the evidence quiet to follow the money, or leak now. **Cliffhanger:** the retainer's recipient field is cropped. Biko takes your phone, says "I'll
fix the crop", and gives it back with a smile you have never seen on him.

**Ch 8 — Lights Out. (NEW, the midpoint set piece.)** *Setting:* a city-wide blackout. Your phone is at 9% and someone is following the page. *Special rule:* **the Blackout**: Social channels run at half power for the whole case, the Heat starts at 30,
and three scripted events hit on days 5, 9 and 13: a power cut at the generator house, a car that has been behind you for six streets, and a locked door. **Reversal (the Twist B reveal):** Biko is outside HRP's office in the CCTV still (C12). He has been on their payroll
since month one (C11). **The choice that shapes the season:** **Fire him, forgive him, or use him** (feed him false stories to send back). This sets Trust (0 to 10). **Cliffhanger:** *"I never told them anything that hurt you, boss. Until Thursday."*

### ACT 3 — HOME (Ch 9 to 11): the betrayals were arranged
**Ch 9 — The Aunties.** *Target:* your mother-in-law. *Twist:* **a whole family against you**: a bot-swarm of cousins and a Hometown region (a "family" region of the map starts at high Doubt). **Reversal:** the notebook she keeps is a ledger: every
payment she received from "H.R.P.", for six years, starting the month you left Halo. **Receipt:** C13. **Choice:** confront her with the notebook at the family meeting, or quietly photograph it. **Cliffhanger:** the last page of the notebook is a
list of names with ticks. Yours is at the bottom. It is not ticked.

**Ch 10 — Fine Print.** *Target:* your spouse (you pick). *Twist:* **a legal thriller**: the Target hits with Injunctions and Evidence Raids. **Reversal:** a clause in your marriage papers was added after signing, by a lawyer on HRP's panel. **Receipt:** C14.
**Choice:** use the clause in public (humiliates you both) or privately offer them a way out. **Cliffhanger:** your spouse: *"I did not leave you. I was told what would happen if I stayed."*

**Ch 11 — The Compound.** *Target:* your uncle, the landlord. *Twist:* **the homecoming**: a short clock, and the compound's elders as a Hometown region. **Reversal (Twist A begins):** the backdated deed (C15) is notarised by an HRP lawyer, and beneath it
is a page of Halo's ledger (C16): *"Subject: THE RECEIPTS. Project Mirror, year 4. Status: on track."* Every person you have exposed was part of it. **Cliffhanger:** the ledger line at the bottom, in Halo's handwriting: *"She will find this chapter first. That is the point."*

### ACT 4 — THE FACTORY (Ch 12 to 14): the plan
**Ch 12 — The Factory.** *Target:* Madam Ngozi. *Twist:* **infiltration**: the Target is the one with the passwords; **Evidence is the whole game** (verified evidence is 40% cheaper to leak, Forgeries are disabled: you cannot lie in a room full of liars). **Reversal:** the "Factory" is a floor of 30 young writers, each
writing apologies for the powerful. Fola is one of them. The Oracle was never a hacker. She is an employee. **Receipt:** C17, C18. **Choice:** take Ngozi's drive (and her with it) or leave her a way to live with herself. **Cliffhanger:** on the drive, one folder named *CHAPTER 16*.

**Ch 13 — The Client.** *Target:* Chief Mbakwe. *Twist:* **the big man's machine**: tier-5 pressure with a Watched play style, and a scheduled raid by his security on day 12. **Reversal:** his campaign calendar (C19) has one line: *Election Eve, 6:06 PM: the Receipt.* The plan is not to expose a rival. It
is to have **you** post a fabricated receipt about the opposing candidate, because the public believes you. **Receipt:** C19. **Choice:** pretend to cooperate (go undercover) or refuse and run. **Cliffhanger:** a voice note from Halo, warm as ever: *"I knew you would find it. Come to the wedding. Peace and light."*

**Ch 14 — The Owambe. (NEW, the heist.)** *Setting:* Chief Mbakwe's daughter's wedding in Ikoyi: 800 guests, three bands, one safe. You are inside to take the master file. *Special rule:* **the Heist**: the case has a very short clock (14 days), Heat is the enemy, and the event schedule is a
timetable (the first dance, the speeches, the spraying of money). Each event is a window to act. Cameo-rich: the party DJ, the pastor, the skit-maker, all filming. **Reversal:** the file you steal contains your *own* fabricated receipt, already written in your voice (C20), plus a signed donor list (C21).
**Choice:** leak the donor list now (a public scandal, but the plan goes ahead with another page) or hold it for the real fight. **Cliffhanger:** Biko, at the door with the Inspector: *"Boss, run."*

### ACT 5 — PEACE AND LIGHT (Ch 15 to 16): the finale
**Ch 15 — Six-Oh-Six. (NEW.)** *The countdown:* 48 hours to election eve. Your page is hacked, your accounts frozen, and everyone you have exposed is coming back to say you lied. *Special rule:* **the Siege**: a hard 12-day clock, an Evidence Raid every four days, and the player's own channels start
**already** being flooded with Spin. **Reversal:** Inspector Coker's case file (C22) shows he has known who you are for months, and has not arrested you. He wanted the plan, not the page. He gives you a choice. **Choices (these set the finale):** partner with the Inspector, partner with the journalist Zainab,
or go alone. **Cliffhanger:** your phone buzzes at 6:06. A new statement: *"I want to address the allegations against Halo."* It is in your voice.

**Ch 16 — Peace and Light.** *Target:* Dr. Bankole "Halo" Adeyemo. *Twist:* **the Mentor** fights with *your* playbook: every counter he plays is a move you taught him (or he taught you). Phase two begins at 50% Believers: the case flips to a **mirror match** where the Target's powers
copy the channels *you* bought. **Reversal (Twist C):** Halo plays you the recording (C23): you, at 24, writing the first "Peace and light" for a client, to cover the incident. The handwriting is yours (C24). "I did not teach you the line. I stole it." **The final choice** (see Endings).

- **Inspector Coker (the story's Heat):** a hidden counter that rises with reckless leaks and falls when you cooperate. It changes the first sentence of his every scene, and in Chapter 15 it decides whether he is a threat or an ally.
- **Integrity (kept):** the existing reputation number still shapes Doubt and the tone of the ending.
- **The Clue Board:** 24 clues. Players are shown the *count* only. What they have changes the dialogue options in the finale.

## 8. How each chapter plays differently (so it is never "the same match")
The match engine already supports everything below, so each chapter can feel like its scene rather than a reskin:
- **Per-chapter rules:** shorter or longer clocks, starting Heat, disabled lanes or forgeries (Ch 12), a lane that halves.
- **Scripted plot beats** on fixed days (the "natural occurrences" engine built for the Arena): a car behind you on day 9, the Chief's security on day 12, the first dance on day 6.
- **Target moves that match the story:** Injunctions for lawyers, Account Freezes for the money chapters, Region Lockdowns for family compounds, Evidence Raids for the Factory.
- **The mirror match** (Ch 16) is new: phase two copies the channels the player owns into the Target's own powers.
- **Each chapter has a one-line tip** from Biko (or the Oracle, once she is on side) so the player is never stuck.

## 11. How a chapter is presented (the "film" layer)
A **scene** is a short sequence of beats, played full-screen between and inside cases. Beat types:
- **Chat** — a WhatsApp-style thread (typing indicators, "last seen", blue ticks, a message that arrives mid-sentence and changes everything).
- **Voice note** — a waveform with a transcript that types as it "plays", with room tone and a stinger.
- **News** — a ticker or a TV lower-third, the way the country actually finds out.
- **CCTV / still** — a grainy image with a timestamp and a crop that reveals the detail.
- **Document** — a Notes-app apology, a ledger page, a court order, an HR email, with the key line highlighted.
- **Cold open** — black screen, one line of text, the timestamp.
- **Split screen** — two phones, two rooms, same moment.
- **Choice** — a timed decision (with a visible cost), sometimes with a countdown so the player feels the pressure.
Every scene has sound: the existing music engine gets thriller stingers, a ticking bed for countdowns and a silence cue before a reveal. Portraits use the face system with the existing
emotion states, so Halo's smile can slip for one frame. Screen shake on reveals, a vignette that tightens with the Inspector's Heat, and a "previously on…" recap that plays when a player returns after a day.

## 12. Production plan
**What gets built (engine and UI):**
1. **Scene engine and format** (data-driven, like Target packs): beats, timing, choices, sound cues, variables. The writers (you, and me) never touch code to add a scene.
2. **Chapter flow**: prologue, recap, cold open, mid-case beat cards, aftermath, cliffhanger, "next chapter" teaser.
3. **Clue Board** (the corkboard with strings), **Trust** and **Coker** meters, the multi-ending resolver.
4. **Chapter rules**: per-chapter match modifiers and scripted plot-beat events (reusing the Arena's occurrence engine), the Heist and Siege rule sets, the mirror match.
5. **Premium gating** for the three bonus episodes (using the existing licence system).
6. **A "script checker"** (like the Target validator): flags missing clues, unreachable endings, a scene that sets a variable nothing reads.

**What I write:** about 16 cold opens, 16 aftermaths, mid-case beats, 24 clue cards, the finale tree and 5 endings, plus 3 bonus episodes. Roughly 30,000 words, in the voice the story already has.
